# Research the market and competitors

## Formulate a frame

Determine what decision the analysis should support: need, positioning, business model, feature scope, UX model or risk. Don’t put together a catalog of “all competitors” without asking.

Divide:

- direct products for the same task and audience;
- indirect ways to achieve the same progress;
- adjacent categories with useful mechanics;
- internal alternatives and manual user processes.

##Collecting evidence

For each example, save source, date, market, platform, eligibility, pricing/plan, verified flow and access restrictions. Prefer official documentation for stated capabilities and real screens for implementation. Use reviews and testimonials as a separate type of evidence.

## Comparison

Compare one frame at a time:

- user task and moment of use;
- input, main path, states and recovery;
- delegation, control and trust;
- data, permissions and privacy;
- business model and incentives;
- strengths, trade-offs and limitations;
- what is transferable to the current product and what depends on another context.

Don't judge "better/worse" based on the number of features alone. Do not copy branding, texts or visual form. Don’t call a popular pattern effective without outcome evidence.

## Exit

Bring back landscape, comparison matrix, repeatable strategies, whitespace/risks, applicability and questions. Each output is associated with a source/evidence, and the unknown remains unknown.
